# The Musical Beat Board: 40 book beats around 19 songs, from the sources

*Research doc, 2026-09-24. Companion to MUSICAL-BEAT-MAP.md (where the songs
sit) and Colin's `book-musical-card-grid-v3.md` (the craft template). Built
from 20 two-act book musicals, 1943 to 2024, each beat-sheeted from its
published synopsis with Save the Cat's 15 beats. Every song and every spoken
plot event was recorded in order, then measured. Data and scripts:
`corpus/story-beats/`.*

**How to read it.** The board is the app's **Story Board** template: 40 book beats around 19 songs. Its four lanes are Snyder's acts: lane 1 runs to the end of the Debate, lane 2A opens on Break into Two and ends at the Act One curtain, lane 2B runs through the Dark Night, lane 3 opens on Break into Three. That makes the rows uneven (13, 18, 18, 10) on purpose: a musical's Act Three is short. **♪** is a song seat, **¶** a book beat. In the findings, percentages are how far through the evening a beat lands (the median of 20 shows, with every song and spoken event counted as one card), and "14 of 20" counts shows.

---

## What the sources say

1. **Act One is Save the Cat almost to the percent.** Catalyst 10% (Snyder
   11%) · Break into Two 23% (23%) · B Story 25% (27%) · Midpoint 47% (50%).
2. **The back half is not.** When the bottom falls in Act Two, All Is Lost
   lands at 75% (Snyder 68%) and Break into Three at 87% (77%), about three
   cards later. A musical's Act Three is half a screenplay's: the recovery is
   fast and the finale is short.
3. **Events are spoken; responses are sung.** The two beats that happen *to*
   the hero are book: the Catalyst is spoken in 14 of 20, All Is Lost in 15
   of 20. The theme is sung in 15 of 19 (a film says its theme; a musical
   sings it), Fun and Games in 19 of 20, the Midpoint in 15 of 19. The hero's
   own choices split down the middle: Break into Two is sung in 10 of 20,
   Break into Three in 12.
4. **The I Want answers the Catalyst.** It comes after the Catalyst in 11 of
   the 15 shows that have one. In a musical it belongs to the Debate, not the
   Set-Up.
5. **The curtain is a high or a low, never neutral.** Act One ends on the
   Midpoint in 12 shows (9 false victories, 3 false defeats), on the bottom
   pulled forward in 5, and on a cliffhanger in 3. When the bottom comes at
   the curtain, Act Two opens in the Dark Night.
6. **The B Story arrives after the Break into Two in 13 of 20**, at the same
   rate before 1990 (7 of 11) and since (6 of 9).
7. **Bad Guys Close In is the musical's biggest zone.** It starts before the
   curtain in 14 of 20 and fills most of Act Two. Its tool is the reprise: 10
   of the 18 reprises in these shows land here.
8. **The Break into Three turns on a relationship, not a plan**, in 16 of 20
   by this doc's reading of the sheets. It's sung in 12, often as a duet or a
   reprise of an earlier love song. The eleven o'clock sits inside the Finale
   (7 of 15) or the Dark Night (4), rarely at the Break into Three (2).
9. **All Is Lost is a literal death in 7 of 20.**

## Against the card grid (v3)

**Agrees.**
- Catalyst and Break into Two often live in book: the Catalyst is spoken in 14 of 20, and Break into Two splits 10 and 10.
- The Midpoint is the Act One finale in 12 of 20.
- All Is Lost is spoken (15 of 20), and the Dark Night is a solo (12 sung).
- The eleven o'clock fires inside the main event (7 of 15).

**Refines.**
- The curtain can also be the bottom: 5 of 20, including West Side Story, Ragtime and Hairspray.
- The I Want usually comes after the Catalyst (11 of 15). The grid puts it at card 5 and the Catalyst at card 10, which is the minority order.

**Doesn't hold as a rule.**
- Moving the B Story into the new world isn't a contemporary shift. It lands after the Break into Two in 7 of 11 shows before 1990 and 6 of 9 since. The sheets label the theme-carrying relationship, which isn't always the love interest, but the corpus test of love songs found the same thing: deferring love to Act Two is perennial, not generational.
- The false peak, with catastrophe within 1 to 3 cards of the love duet, holds in 4 of 19: Wicked, West Side Story, Into the Woods and Dear Evan Hansen. The usual gap is about 5 cards.

## Method and limits

- **Sample:** Oklahoma!, Guys and Dolls, My Fair Lady, West Side Story, The Music Man, Gypsy, Fiddler on the Roof, Cabaret, Sweeney Todd, Little Shop of Horrors, Into the Woods, Beauty and the Beast, Ragtime, The Producers, Hairspray, Wicked, Billy Elliot, The Book of Mormon, Dear Evan Hansen and The Outsiders. There are no one-acts, films or sung-through shows. Eras were mixed between the two researchers, so labelling habits can't pass for era differences.
- **Sources:** Wikipedia plot sections, cross-checked where possible. StageAgent's scene pages were gated. Every sheet is in own-words summary: at most 15 words per line, and no lyrics.
- **Clocks:** the card clock counts every unit equally. The corpus song-minute clock runs 0 to 7 points later for every beat, because it can't see spoken time.
- **Limits:**
  - Beat labels are one reading per show. Gypsy and Cabaret are medium confidence.
  - Book beats were logged sparsely and unevenly: 2 in Ragtime, 20 in The Music Man. That's why several book cards are marked *Arranged*.
- **Reproduce:** `node corpus/story-beats/board.mjs` (consensus board, curtains, the B Story's placement, who sings each beat) and `node corpus/story-beats/checks.mjs` (song function against beat, finale sub-beats, the false peak).
